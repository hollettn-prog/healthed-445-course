Learning Materials

From goals to evidence of learning

Think about a lesson where students can name three signs that a health claim might be misleading. That's useful content, but it doesn't tell you whether they can evaluate a claim when they encounter one.

Start with what students will do

Choose a grades 9 to 12 NHES performance indicator. Look closely at its action verb. Are students expected to analyze, demonstrate, evaluate, or advocate? Your learning goal should describe a performance you could actually observe.

Match the task to the goal

Suppose your goal is for students to evaluate the credibility of a consumer health claim. Asking them to define credibility checks memory. Asking them to examine a sample claim, compare sources, and justify a decision gives you stronger evidence of the skill.

Decide what counts as evidence

What would you need to see in each student's response? Look for the reasoning behind the decision, not just the final answer. Consider whether your task lets every student show their own thinking.

Try it

  1. Choose a Consumer Health context and one relevant NHES performance indicator.
  2. Write one learning goal using an observable action.
  3. Describe a brief task that would let a student demonstrate that action.
  4. Name two things you would look for in an individual student's work.

You'll use this thinking in the Module 2 discussion.

Willie's Teaching Tip

Does your task measure the skill in your goal, or mainly whether students remember the content?
