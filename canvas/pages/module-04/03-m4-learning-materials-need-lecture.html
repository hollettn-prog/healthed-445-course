The Classroom Case

Ms. Rivera is teaching a fictional eighth-grade lesson on stress and help-seeking. Students will practice making a respectful request for support in a fictional school situation.

Student task: Choose an appropriate adult in the situation, explain the concern, and make a clear request for help. Each student provides their own spoken or written request.

Students may use fictional examples throughout. They do not need to share personal experiences. The school’s actual referral and reporting procedures are not supplied in this case.

Three Moments to Plan For

A. A Harmful Comment

During partner practice, a student says, “People who need help with stress are weak.” Several classmates hear it. Alex stops contributing.

B. A Participation Barrier

Asked to describe a time they needed help, a student says, “I don’t want to talk about my life.” The learning goal is to practice a support request; it does not require a personal story.

C. A Private Concern

A student asks to speak privately and adds, “Only if you promise not to tell anyone.” The student has not explained the concern.

As You Work

Separate what you can observe from what you are assuming. Plan the words you would say, your next action, and what you would check afterward.

For the private concern, plan a supportive response without promising secrecy or guessing what the student will disclose. Identify the school procedures and support roles you would need to verify before acting.
