Progression Across Lessons

A change from a poster to a video does not by itself explain what students learn next. Name the developing reasoning and the evidence you will use. Keep necessary access supports while reducing worked answers and prompts that supply the reasoning.

Unit Context and Goal

Continue with the fictional eighth-grade Consumer Health profile and source cards in M5. Students evaluate an unfamiliar wellness post using at least two relevant credibility considerations, connect details to a justified judgment, and explain meaningful limitations. This is selected source-evaluation work within Standard 3, not mastery of the full standard.

Additional Fictional Source Cards

Lesson 2 report: A planner company reports that 13 of 18 volunteers felt more organized after using its product for two weeks. The report is company-funded, has no comparison group, and promotes the planner. The finding concerns reported organization; it does not establish that the product caused a change or reduces stress for everyone.

Lesson 3: CalmTrack Planner: An influencer promises “zero school stress in seven days” and supplies a paid purchase code. The card includes no research supporting that promise. Students receive this information and the shared criteria, but no worked evaluation of this particular post. These claims are fictional materials to evaluate, not health advice.

Worked Evidence-to-Action Decision

If learners list a creator and date but cannot explain why they matter, plan another brief detail-to-judgment model before increasing complexity. If they connect details to a judgment but overstate certainty, focus on limits. If the work already meets the current criteria, plan a more demanding application. These are anticipated decisions; do not invent results for an untaught unit.

Access Across the Unit

Keep source cards, readable text, read-aloud options, and appropriate oral responses available. Remove the worked conclusion when you need to see independent reasoning. Carry relevant M4 routines into partner work and transitions. Include the actual task materials, not only titles or external links.
