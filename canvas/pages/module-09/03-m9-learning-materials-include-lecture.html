A Reflection Needs Evidence

Choose a claim you can support with your work. Point to a specific section, explain the teaching decision, and acknowledge what you still do not know. A revised plan shows planning and revision; it does not establish a change in school learners’ performance.

Morgan’s Reflection Case

Morgan revised an accessible lesson plan using peer feedback but has not taught it. In this fictional example, the earlier task used an image of a source card and required a long written paragraph. Morgan added selectable text and an oral response option while keeping the same evaluation criteria.

Two possible reflection claims
ClaimWhat the evidence supports
“My revision improved students’ evaluation skills.”No learner results are available. The claim goes beyond the supplied evidence.
“I revised the source materials and response option to address an access barrier.”The original section, feedback, and revised section can support this planning claim.

A Useful Explanation

“My original plan required students to interpret an image of a post and write a long response. Peer feedback identified a reading and writing barrier. I added selectable source text and an oral option using the same criteria. This shows how I revised access in my plan. I would need individual learner responses to examine how it works in teaching.”

A Practical Next Step

Choose an action you could carry out when the opportunity exists. For example: teach the revised task and examine individual responses with its criteria. Say what you would collect and what finding would lead you to revise. A general intention to be more confident is harder to evaluate.
