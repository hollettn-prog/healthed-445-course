Ms. Rivera’s Class

In this fictional example, Ms. Rivera’s 26 eighth graders can recall Disease Prevention practices. They need practice making a decision when the usual routine or supplies change. She has one 45-minute class, printed cases, and the information card below.

Student goal: Given a fictional school situation and the health-information card, students will compare two possible responses, explain a likely consequence of each, and justify a feasible choice that supports health.

Shared Information Card

Health information: Washing hands with soap and water helps remove germs. Before eating is an important time to wash hands.

Procedures for these fictional cases: Students stay with their group unless an adult authorizes another arrangement. A supervising adult can help with missing supplies, an accessible washing station, or timing. Students do not need to solve a supply or access problem on their own.

The Three Cases

Case A: Your Model

There is a long handwashing line before lunch. A student worries there will be little time left to eat. A supervising adult is nearby. Compare waiting with timing support and asking about an authorized alternative station.

Case B: Guided Practice

Before snack, students discover an empty soap dispenser. A supervising adult can request a refill or arrange another washing station. Partners compare two feasible responses and explain a likely consequence of each before choosing.

Case C: Individual Application

A room change causes the class to miss its usual handwashing stop before lunch. A supervising adult can arrange a nearby station and adjust timing. Each student compares two feasible responses, explains likely consequences, and justifies a choice.

Standards and Health Reference

This example addresses selected parts of Standard 5, especially NHES 5.8.3–5.8.5. It does not establish mastery of the full standard or consistent behavior outside class.
