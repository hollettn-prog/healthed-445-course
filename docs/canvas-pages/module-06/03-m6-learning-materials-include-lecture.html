A Response Needs a Check

An unfinished answer does not tell you why it is unfinished. Describe what the learner said or did, ask a focused clarifying question, and choose a response based on what you learn. Then collect a fresh individual application. These are working interpretations, not fixed labels for students.

Environmental Health Case

Ms. Rivera’s fictional class has 26 eighth graders in a 45-minute lesson; 20 minutes remain. Students recommend a feasible school response to unnecessary vehicle idling, explain its connection to emissions, and identify a barrier or support adults should address.

Information card: Idling means a parked vehicle’s engine is running. A combustion engine can emit exhaust while parked. Reducing unnecessary idling can reduce emissions. In this fictional case, adults review operating, safety, and access needs; learners do not operate vehicles or direct traffic.

Three Responses

Use the response before choosing a teaching move
LearnerEvidence supplied
A“The bus only makes pollution while it’s moving.”
B“Ask transportation staff to review unnecessary idling. A running engine can produce exhaust while parked. Staff should check operating needs and provide an accessible waiting arrangement.”
C“The word idling confused me. Can I explain out loud?” The student has not finished writing.

Worked Example: Student C

Clarify idling and ask C to explain a recommendation orally. If the reasoning is present, provide a way to record that same reasoning. If the explanation still misses the engine/exhaust connection, teach that specific gap. An access barrier and a conceptual gap can occur together.

For A, a useful check asks what happens when a bus is parked with its combustion engine running. For B, an extension can add an access problem: the proposed waiting area has stairs and no accessible route. Ask what staff would need to verify or change. Keep the other learners working with clear directions while you confer.

A Fresh Individual Check

Use this new fictional situation: at an evening school event, cars with running combustion engines wait near an entrance. Administrators are reviewing arrival procedures. Each student recommends a feasible response, explains the connection to emissions, and identifies a barrier adults should address. Keep the information card and needed access supports available.

Time update: Only six minutes remain and individual recheck evidence has not been collected. Decide what to preserve, shorten, or defer. Specify how you will obtain any evidence that remains unavailable. Do not invent improved results.
