Two Approaches to the Same Topic

In this fictional ninth-grade lesson, students evaluate health claims on social media. Their goal is to compare sources and justify which is more trustworthy using details about the creator, supporting evidence, and purpose.

Comparison of student tasks and evidence
Dimension Content-Focused Skills-Based
Teacher actions Explains how to examine a source’s creator, evidence, and purpose. Uses the same content and thinks aloud while comparing two fictional posts.
Student actions Takes notes and identifies the three considerations on a recall quiz. Compares posts with a partner, explains a judgment, and revises after feedback.
Individual check Recalls what to consider when examining a source. Evaluates a different pair of posts using supplied source details and justifies a judgment.
What the work shows Recall of the considerations. More evidence is needed to judge application. How the learner uses source evidence on this task and where their reasoning needs more practice.

Both approaches can support learning. What matters is whether the task and evidence match the goal. A worksheet, discussion, or quiz can involve recall or application. Look at what students actually do.

Think it through: A student writes, “I trust this post because it looks professional.” What does that reveal? What would you ask the student to examine next?

Connect to the Frameworks

Connect this example to Benes and Alperin's skills-based approach. How does relevant health content support what students practice?

Consult Standard 3 in the 2024 National Health Education Standards. Consider how evaluating resources differs from recalling facts about them.
